A compulsory injury arrangement and a policy that repairs a damaged EV are not the same protection. Confusing them can leave a large gap between the insurance document you recognise and the bill you expect someone to pay.

Australia and New Zealand make that distinction especially useful to understand. Pacific island markets add another reason not to treat a regional label as a single insurance system.

**Short answer:** Check injury arrangements and motor property protection separately, then ask how the actual EV is insured. This guide mainly discusses Australia and New Zealand, with a caution about independent Pacific-market supervision. It does not establish every Pacific country's requirements or provide a region-wide premium. [1][2][3]

## Establish the country and the relevant local arrangement

Begin with registration, where the EV is normally kept, its drivers and its use. For an Australian vehicle, also identify the relevant state or territory instead of assuming every process is identical.

For New Zealand, use New Zealand sources and your contract. Do not transfer an Australian compulsory-insurance term into a New Zealand policy without checking what it means locally.

The [homepage regional selector](/#eh-map) groups countries and areas for navigation. It does not establish that every jurisdiction in Oceania has the same legal requirements, products or complaint process.

If you are importing a vehicle, moving or planning travel, explain those circumstances early. A guide about ordinary local use cannot confirm acceptance of a different arrangement.

## Separate injury protection from vehicle and property protection

ASIC's Moneysmart distinguishes several forms of car insurance, including compulsory third-party injury protection, third-party property protection and comprehensive insurance. New Zealand consumer guidance separately explains motor cover. [1][2]

| Question | Australia example | New Zealand example |
|---|---|---|
| What addresses injury? | Check the relevant compulsory injury arrangement | Understand ACC's accident-injury role |
| What addresses another vehicle or property? | Read the selected property protection | Read the selected motor policy |
| What protects my own EV? | Check the contract and insured events | Check the contract and insured events |
| How is value determined? | Read the settlement basis | Distinguish market and agreed value where offered |
| What happens during repairs? | Check transport and related benefits separately | Check transport and related benefits separately |

The table is a question framework, not a national schedule of benefits. ACC's injury role and the local insurance documents need to be read separately. [2][3]

## Australia: CTP does not mean comprehensive car protection

Moneysmart explains the distinction between compulsory third-party insurance and protection for property or the insured vehicle. Do not treat the presence of the word “third party” as enough to identify what a policy pays. [1]

Ask which separate coverage addresses accidental damage to your EV or damage you cause to another vehicle or property. Check the excess, insured events and exclusions.

For an EV, identify the exact vehicle and ask how battery assessment and repairs would be authorised. A general explanation of car-insurance types does not guarantee a particular battery replacement.

Keep the policy wording and schedule together. A registration-related insurance payment, a motor-insurance quotation and a manufacturer warranty are different records with different purposes.

## State examples: compare South Australia and New South Wales carefully

South Australia's CTP Regulator describes its injury-protection role and explains that CTP is paid with vehicle-registration renewal there. That payment arrangement should not be assumed to describe every Australian jurisdiction. [4]

SIRA's New South Wales information describes a Green Slip as personal-injury insurance needed before registration. Its guidance addresses injury protection rather than a promise to repair your own EV. [5]

These examples show why the state label matters. Ask the responsible local source about the required arrangement, then check property insurance independently.

For a practical Australian enquiry, write down the state or territory before listing your questions. A suggested message is: “Please distinguish the injury arrangement from the motor-property protection offered for my EV. Which document covers damage to my car, which excess applies, and what approval is needed before diagnostic or battery work?”

This is a sample request, not an example of an accepted claim. Ask the insurer to identify the relevant policy sections and any additional information it needs.

Then check that the vehicle description, drivers and use match the circumstances you supplied. If the response refers only to the registration-related arrangement, do not treat it as an answer about comprehensive protection. Ask the own-vehicle question again and keep the written response.

This guide does not reproduce every state's claims rules or current benefits. Use the applicable authority when a deadline, entitlement or document requirement needs a precise answer.

## New Zealand: ACC and motor property insurance answer different questions

ACC describes a no-fault scheme for qualifying accident injuries in New Zealand. That injury framework is not a motor-policy promise to repair a damaged car. [3]

New Zealand Consumer Protection explains cover for vehicle damage, theft and property risks. Use the motor-policy documents to identify the protection actually selected. [2]

Ask separately what happens to your own EV, another person's vehicle and any recovery or temporary transport expenses. Do not assume one arrangement answers all of those questions.

If the vehicle's use or circumstances change, ask what information the provider needs. Keep the answer with the policy instead of relying on an assumption made when the car was first purchased.

## Read market value and agreed value as contract terms

New Zealand Consumer Protection and the Insurance Council of New Zealand explain market-value and agreed-value arrangements. They are not interchangeable labels for the vehicle's purchase price or remaining debt. [2][6]

Ask which basis applies, how the schedule records it and what deductions or conditions may affect a settlement. Do not assume an agreed figure removes every other policy condition.

For an imported or unusual EV, ask how its specification, condition and relevant equipment are documented. A calculation based on a guessed value is not an insurer's accepted assessment.

Keep any valuation evidence dated. If the vehicle is financed, ask the lender separately about the agreement; the balance owed does not itself establish the motor-policy payment.

## Pacific markets need their own verification

Oceania is wider than Australia and New Zealand. A regional navigation link should not silently export either country's insurance arrangements to Pacific island markets.

Fiji's Reserve Bank publishes its own insurance annual report covering the local industry and supervision. That is a useful reminder to identify the relevant local institution rather than assume Australian or New Zealand oversight. [7]

This article does not establish Fiji's motor-insurance minimums or extend its example to other islands. Ask the responsible local authority and proposed insurer about registration, accepted cover and any required certificate.

For a vehicle brought from another market, explain the specification, ownership and intended use. Ask about acceptance, assessment, repair arrangements and territorial conditions before depending on the policy.

## Ask how batteries, charging equipment and repair time are handled

Describe the cause of a potential loss. Accident damage, a suspected defect and ordinary capacity decline should not be combined into a single question about whether “the battery” is covered.

Our [battery insurance and warranty guide](/blog/ev-battery-insurance-warranty-replacement-cost-guide/) helps organise the distinction. It does not determine an Australian, New Zealand or Pacific insurer's decision.

For charging equipment, identify ownership and installation details. The [home-charger guide](/blog/does-home-insurance-cover-ev-charger/) is a useful question list, not a universal rule allocating costs between home and motor insurance.

Ask who authorises inspections, parts and additional work, and how temporary transport or storage is funded. The [repair-delay guide](/blog/ev-insurance-repair-delays-rental-storage/) helps keep those timelines separate without inventing local benefits.

## Compare the offer and organise the claim record

Request matching vehicle details, use, drivers, policy period, currency, excesses and optional protection when comparing quotes. Record differences before deciding that a lower annual figure is a better match.

Use our [EV quote-comparison checklist](/blog/how-to-compare-ev-insurance-quotes/) to organise the terms. The homepage comparator does not retrieve local premiums or convert Australian and New Zealand dollars.

Keep the policy, schedule, endorsements and relevant evidence securely. Our [claim-document checklist](/blog/ev-insurance-claim-documents-checklist/) provides a structure, but local reporting duties and deadlines still need checking.

If a trip or relocation is involved, name the destination and ask how liability, own damage, recovery and approved repairs are addressed. Regional membership does not itself establish territorial cover.

## Know the applicable complaint route

AFCA explains the insurance complaints it can consider in Australia and the circumstances and limits of its process. Use its current guidance rather than assuming every disagreement is automatically eligible. [8]

Do not treat AFCA as the complaints body for every country in Oceania. For a New Zealand or Pacific policy, identify the procedure applicable to that provider and product.

Ask the insurer for a clear written position and the relevant policy section. Keep dates, reports and correspondence so the question is understandable if it needs further review.

Before paying or renewing, leave with an identifiable insurer, an accurate vehicle description and an explanation of the selected protection. Those records are more useful than a broad statement that the car is comprehensively insured.

## Frequently asked questions

**Does Australian CTP repair my damaged EV?**

Do not confuse compulsory injury protection with motor property cover. Moneysmart distinguishes those categories; check the separate protection selected for the vehicle. [1]

**Does every Australian state collect CTP in the same way?**

Do not assume so. The South Australian and New South Wales sources describe their own arrangements. Use the applicable local authority. [4][5]

**Does ACC establish payment for a New Zealand car repair?**

No such conclusion follows from ACC's accident-injury role. Use the actual motor-policy documents for vehicle and property protection. [2][3]

**Is agreed value the same thing as my loan balance?**

No. The motor-policy valuation basis and the amount owed under a finance agreement are different questions. Read the contract and ask the lender separately. [2][6]

**Does this guide state the motor-insurance law of every Pacific island?**

No. It flags the need for local verification and cites Fiji's separate supervision as an example. It does not extend Australian or New Zealand rules throughout the Pacific. [7]

**Does comprehensive insurance guarantee every battery problem?**

Do not assume so. Identify the cause of loss and ask which contract and exclusions apply. This regional guide does not approve a claim or replace a warranty.

**Can everyone in Oceania use AFCA for a dispute?**

No. AFCA's cited process concerns eligible complaints within its scope in Australia. Check the correct local process for another country and provider. [8]
