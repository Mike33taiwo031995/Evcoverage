Electric vehicles (EVs) can cost less to maintain than gasoline cars, but when something breaks or an accident causes damage, the repair bill can be higher than owners expect. The reason is the technology: EVs drop many engine parts but add high-voltage batteries, electric motors, power electronics, and software that can call for specialized diagnostics, manufacturer procedures, and parts that are not always readily available.

**Short answer:** on average, yes, but the gap is narrowing. In the second quarter of 2026, repairable battery-electric vehicle (BEV) collision claims in the U.S. averaged $5,684, versus $4,955 for gasoline vehicles, a difference of $729, according to [Mitchell's Q2 2026 EV Collision Insights report](https://www.mitchell.com/insights/article/auto-physical-damage/plugged-in-ev-collision-insights-q2-2026). Routine upkeep runs the other way: a U.S. Department of Energy analysis estimated scheduled maintenance at 6.1 cents per mile for a battery-electric vehicle versus 10.1 cents for a gasoline vehicle.

Those are averages, not quotes. They describe repairable claims in one dataset, not the bill any individual owner should expect. The useful question is which repairs are likely, what drives their cost, and which insurance coverage may help.

## 1. Why electric vehicle repairs can be expensive

An EV has a different mechanical and electrical architecture from a gasoline car. That changes how technicians diagnose damage and what a repair involves.

### High-voltage battery systems

The traction battery stores the energy that powers the electric motor. It contains interconnected cells, electrical connections, monitoring hardware, and a battery-management system.

Damage to the battery enclosure, cooling system, connections, or internal cells can require extensive investigation. A technician has to decide whether the problem can be repaired safely, whether particular components can be replaced, or whether a larger assembly needs replacement.

Battery replacement is not automatically necessary after every accident. The right repair depends on the manufacturer's procedures, the location and severity of the damage, diagnostic findings, and the availability of approved repair methods. For typical out-of-warranty pack prices, see our guide to [EV battery replacement costs in 2026](/blog/ev-battery-replacement-cost-2026/).

The National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA) advises vehicle owners not to attempt to repair a damaged electric or hybrid-electric vehicle themselves, and to contact an authorized service center or the vehicle manufacturer instead.

### Specialized technicians and diagnostic equipment

EV repair work can involve more than swapping a damaged part. A repair facility may need to:

- Scan vehicle systems for diagnostic trouble codes.
- Inspect high-voltage components using manufacturer-approved procedures.
- Isolate the high-voltage system before certain repairs.
- Test electrical connections and cooling systems.
- Reprogram modules or complete software procedures.
- Calibrate cameras, radar, or other driver-assistance systems after a collision repair.

Not every repair needs every step. It depends on the vehicle, the fault, and the work performed.

Driver-assistance calibration is a good example of hidden cost, and it is not unique to EVs. In AAA's 2023 research on three model-year 2023 vehicles, replacing driver-assistance components averaged $1,540 (13.2% of the total) in a minor front collision repair, and $684 (40.9% of a $1,698 estimate) in a minor rear collision repair, as [AAA reported](https://newsroom.aaa.com/2023/12/fixing-advanced-vehicle-systems-makes-up-over-one-third-of-repair-costs-following-a-crash/).

### Parts availability

A repair can take longer when a replacement component is unavailable, and the car may sit until a specific part arrives. Mitchell's 2026 analysis found that original equipment manufacturer (OEM) parts made up 85% of the parts dollars specified on estimates for repairable battery-electric vehicles, versus 61% for gasoline vehicles, as [Aftermarket Matters reported](https://www.aftermarketmatters.com/national-news/collision-repair-cost-gap-between-evs-and-ice-vehicles-falls-to-record-low/). That measures what estimates specified, not proof that every EV repair must use OEM parts.

**What this means for owners:** ask the repair facility whether the estimate depends on parts that are currently available, whether approved alternatives exist, and whether further diagnostic work could change the price.

## 2. Common electric vehicle repairs and their cost drivers

No single price list describes EV repairs across all makes and models. Costs vary with the vehicle's age and price, the severity of the damage, labor rates, parts availability, repair procedures, and location. This table shows common categories and why they can become expensive.

| Repair category | What may be involved | Main cost drivers |
|---|---|---|
| Tires and wheel alignment | Tire replacement, suspension inspection, alignment | Tire specifications, wheel damage, suspension condition |
| Brakes | Pads, rotors, calipers, hydraulic components | Corrosion, wear, component condition, labor |
| Suspension and steering | Control arms, shocks, steering components | Impact severity, parts, labor |
| Body and collision damage | Bumpers, doors, body panels, paintwork | Materials, sensors, structural damage, refinishing |
| Cameras and driver-assistance systems | Sensor inspection and calibration | Manufacturer procedures, equipment, diagnostic labor |
| Charging equipment | Charging port, wiring, onboard charging components | Fault location, component availability, electrical diagnosis |
| Thermal management | Coolant leaks, pumps, valves, cooling components | System complexity, access, required procedures |
| High-voltage battery | Inspection, diagnosis, approved component or pack repair | Battery condition, repairability, parts and labor |
| Electric motor and power electronics | Motor, inverter, converter, related electronics | Fault severity, component replacement, diagnostics |

These categories are illustrative. They do not guarantee that a particular repair is available or priced the same way for every vehicle.

### Are EV tires more expensive?

Tire cost depends on the tire size, specification, brand, and replacement options for your vehicle. Weight, torque, driving style, tire pressure, and wheel alignment can all influence wear, but tire life varies widely between vehicles and drivers, so it would be inaccurate to say every EV wears tires at a fixed, higher rate. Follow the manufacturer's recommendations, replace tires with suitable specifications, and check pressure regularly.

### Do electric vehicles need fewer brake repairs?

Regenerative braking uses the electric motor to recover energy while slowing the vehicle, which can reduce reliance on the friction brakes and their wear. Friction brakes still need inspection, and they can corrode or develop other problems when used infrequently. Less routine maintenance does not mean an EV is immune to repairs.

## 3. Electric vehicle maintenance costs versus repair costs

The two terms are often mixed up, but they are different expenses.

**Maintenance** is routine work to keep a vehicle operating properly, such as tire rotation, brake inspections, cabin air-filter replacement, and manufacturer-specified coolant checks.

**Repair** corrects a fault or damage that has already happened, such as replacing a failed charging component, fixing collision damage, or addressing a damaged suspension.

EVs can have lower scheduled maintenance costs because they have no engine oil to change and fewer conventional powertrain parts to service. The U.S. Department of Energy [estimated in June 2021](https://www.energy.gov/eere/vehicles/articles/fotw-1190-june-14-2021-battery-electric-vehicles-have-lower-scheduled) that scheduled maintenance for a light-duty battery-electric vehicle totals 6.1 cents per mile, against 10.1 cents per mile for a conventional gasoline vehicle. That is a historical estimate, not a current price or a forecast for any one owner.

The distinction matters because cheaper routine servicing does not automatically mean cheaper collision repairs. An owner could spend little on scheduled maintenance for years and still face a large bill after a collision damages body panels, suspension, sensors, or a battery enclosure. Budget for both categories rather than assuming savings on one will cover the other.

## 4. How much does electric vehicle repair cost in 2026?

The best starting point is to separate industry-wide claim data from an individual repair estimate. Mitchell's report, published August 20, 2026, is a useful benchmark for U.S. repairable collision claims.

| Vehicle category | Average repairable claim severity, Q2 2026 |
|---|---|
| Battery-electric vehicles (BEVs) | $5,684 |
| Plug-in hybrid electric vehicles (PHEVs) | $5,022 |
| Mild hybrid electric vehicles (MHEVs) | $5,033 |
| Internal-combustion-engine vehicles (ICE) | $4,955 |

*Source: Mitchell, Plugged-In: EV Collision Insights Q2 2026, August 20, 2026.*

The BEV figure was $729 higher than the gasoline figure. Three cautions apply:

- **Repairable claims only.** These are averages for claims that were repaired, not for every accident. Vehicles that are totaled are not in the average, and a small repair can cost far less while serious structural or battery damage can cost much more. Mitchell attributes the narrowing gap partly to higher total-loss frequency and a maturing EV fleet, as [Claims Journal reported](https://www.claimsjournal.com/news/national/2026/08/20/339551.htm).
- **Not maintenance or lifetime cost.** The figures say nothing about scheduled maintenance, total ownership cost, or the price of replacing a whole traction battery.
- **Mix of vehicles.** Claim severity reflects which vehicles and repairs are in the data. [Autobody News](https://www.autobodynews.com/regional/midwest-regional-news/bev-repair-cost-premium-hits-record-low-as-mitchell-warns-of-parts-volatility) reported that the Tesla Model Y and Model 3 together made up more than half of U.S. repairable BEV claims in the quarter, so the average leans toward those models. Don't treat it as a quote for another make, model, or shop.

### Why is the gap changing?

The same coverage reported that Mitchell's Q1 2026 figures were $6,042 for BEVs and $4,902 for gasoline vehicles, a $1,140 gap, so the gap shrank by more than a third in one quarter, mostly because BEV severity fell. A narrowing gap is encouraging, but it doesn't make EV repairs cheap. Mitchell also warned that trade and geopolitical conditions could put renewed pressure on repair costs. Battery systems, specialized parts, diagnostic requirements, and the availability of suitable repair facilities can still make certain jobs expensive.

When shopping for an EV, look at likely repair costs for that particular model rather than relying on one industry average.

## 5. Does car insurance cover electric vehicle repairs?

Sometimes. It depends on the cause of the damage, your policy, its exclusions, your deductible, and the circumstances of the loss. A policy that covers collision damage may not cover every mechanical failure.

### Collision coverage

Collision coverage generally applies to covered damage to your own vehicle from a collision, subject to the policy's terms. If you hit another vehicle or a stationary object, it may help pay for covered repairs after the deductible. If a collision damages the battery enclosure or related components, those costs may be part of the claim, but the insurer and repair professionals must establish the extent of the damage and the right repair method. Collision coverage does not mean every battery problem is covered.

### Comprehensive coverage

Comprehensive coverage generally applies to certain non-collision losses, subject to the policy's terms. Depending on the circumstances, those may include theft, vandalism, hail, falling objects, or certain fires. If a covered fire damages your parked EV, comprehensive coverage may apply. If a charging-related incident causes damage, coverage depends on the cause, the policy wording, and exclusions. For equipment at home, see our guide on [whether home insurance covers an EV charger](/blog/does-home-insurance-cover-ev-charger/).

### Liability coverage

Property-damage liability generally pays for covered damage you cause to someone else's property when you are legally responsible, up to your limits. It does not ordinarily pay to repair your own vehicle. To protect your own EV, you generally need to consider collision and comprehensive coverage.

### Mechanical breakdown and battery degradation

Ordinary mechanical failure and gradual battery degradation are different from accidental damage. Standard auto insurance generally does not work as a maintenance contract or a warranty against wear and tear. A battery that slowly loses usable capacity as it ages is a different matter from one damaged in a covered collision, and the warranty, the insurance policy, and the manufacturer's terms must be read separately. Our guides explain the difference in detail: [EV battery warranty vs. insurance](/blog/ev-battery-warranty-vs-insurance/) and [whether insurance covers EV battery degradation](/blog/does-insurance-cover-ev-battery-degradation/).

**Important:** never assume a repair is covered just because the vehicle is insured. Confirm the coverage and deductible with your insurer before authorizing work.

## 6. Who pays when an EV battery is damaged?

Battery damage is one of the most expensive repair worries for EV owners, but the outcome depends on the facts. Consider three scenarios.

### Scenario A: battery damage after a collision

Suppose an EV strikes road debris and the impact damages the battery enclosure. If the loss is covered under collision coverage, the insurer may pay covered repair costs after the deductible, subject to the policy and the claim decision. The repair facility may need to inspect the enclosure, electrical systems, and other components before deciding what work is needed. Ask for an explanation of the diagnosis, the proposed repair method, and any extra work that may be required. Our guide to [whether insurance covers EV battery replacement](/blog/is-ev-battery-replacement-covered-by-insurance/) goes deeper.

### Scenario B: gradual loss of battery capacity

Suppose range declines over several years. That doesn't automatically mean the battery is defective or eligible for replacement, because capacity can change with age, temperature, use, and other conditions. The manufacturer may provide a battery warranty with specific time, mileage, and capacity-retention terms, so check the warranty for your exact model and model year. Insurance should not be treated as a substitute for that warranty.

### Scenario C: battery damage after flooding

Flooding creates a different set of risks. NHTSA's interim guidance for vehicle owners says a high-voltage battery in a flooded vehicle may have high voltage and short circuits that can shock and cause fires. If you suspect flood damage, don't try to inspect or repair high-voltage components yourself. Contact the manufacturer, an authorized service center, or emergency services where appropriate. Whether flood damage is covered depends on the circumstances, your comprehensive coverage, and the policy's exclusions.

## 7. Can an EV be declared a total loss instead of repaired?

Yes. An insurer may declare an EV a total loss when the legal and policy criteria are met. The decision isn't based only on whether the battery is damaged. It can depend on the estimated repair cost, the vehicle's value, its salvage value, state law, and the insurer's process.

An initial estimate can also change. A first inspection may suggest extensive repairs, and further inspection may reveal more damage to the battery enclosure, suspension, wiring, or structure. If the final estimate meets the total-loss criteria, the claim may be settled under the policy's total-loss provisions instead of through repairs.

This matters financially when the loan balance is higher than the car's value. Gap insurance may help cover an eligible difference between the insurance settlement and the amount owed on a qualifying loan or lease, subject to its terms and exclusions. ## 8. How to choose an EV repair shop

The lowest estimate isn't always the best choice. A good facility has the training, equipment, procedures, and technical information your particular vehicle needs.

### Verify EV-specific qualifications

Ask whether the technicians are trained to work safely on your vehicle's high-voltage systems. NHTSA's guidance is clear that owners shouldn't attempt these repairs themselves, and the same caution applies to any shop that can't explain its EV training.

### Ask about manufacturer repair procedures

Some repairs need model-specific procedures, special tools, or software operations. Ask whether the shop can access the technical information it needs and perform required post-repair inspections and calibrations.

### Request an itemized estimate

A useful estimate separates parts, labor, diagnostic work, and other charges. Ask the shop to explain:

- Which parts will be repaired and which replaced.
- Whether the estimate includes required diagnostic scans.
- Whether driver-assistance sensors need calibration.
- Whether the parts are available now.
- What could cause the estimate to increase.
- Whether the plan follows the manufacturer's procedures.

### Confirm insurance coordination

If the repair is going through an insurance claim, clarify how the insurer and the shop will communicate about the estimate and any supplements. A supplement is an additional estimate or payment request submitted when new damage is found after the vehicle is taken apart, or when the repair plan changes. Supplements are common in collision repair, so ask how they will be handled and approved before work begins.

## 9. Practical ways to reduce EV repair costs

You can't control every repair bill, but these steps help keep avoidable costs down:

- **Keep up with maintenance.** Check tire pressure, keep wheels aligned, and have the brakes inspected on schedule, since friction brakes can corrode if they are rarely used.
- **Know what your warranty covers.** Battery and powertrain warranties can pay for failures that insurance won't. Keep your service records.
- **Review your coverage before you need it.** Compare deductibles, and ask your insurer how it handles EV repairs, including its policy on OEM versus aftermarket parts and whether any optional endorsement applies to repair parts.
- **Get an itemized estimate, and compare when you can.** Rules about choosing your repair facility and getting multiple estimates vary by state and by insurer, so check yours.
- **Document everything.** After an accident, photograph the damage and the scene, keep every estimate, and write down dates and names for each conversation with the insurer and the shop.
- **Pay attention to warnings.** If the dashboard shows a battery or high-voltage warning after an impact, follow the owner's manual and contact a qualified service provider instead of continuing to drive.
