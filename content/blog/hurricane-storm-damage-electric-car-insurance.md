The storm has passed. Your EV is still in the driveway, and from the window it looks mostly intact. The question is not just whether it will start. It is whether approaching it, connecting a charger or trying to move it is safe.

A fallen branch, hail dents and floodwater can all arrive in the same storm. They do not necessarily create the same damage—or the same repair plan.

**Short answer:** Many U.S. auto policies consider windstorm, hail, flood and falling-object damage under comprehensive coverage. The policy must actually include that coverage, and deductibles, exclusions and the facts of the loss still matter. Liability-only cover generally does not pay for storm damage to your own car.

This guide focuses on U.S. personal auto insurance. For a vehicle that was submerged or exposed to floodwater, our [flood-damaged EV insurance guide](/blog/does-car-insurance-cover-flood-damaged-electric-car/) goes deeper into that specific risk.

## First, separate visible damage from electrical safety

Hail on an otherwise unaffected roof is not the same situation as water reaching damaged electrical components. Do not infer the condition of a high-voltage battery from a dry-looking interior or an app showing a charge percentage.

[NHTSA's guidance for damaged electric vehicles](https://www.nhtsa.gov/sites/nhtsa.gov/files/811577-interimguidehev-hv-batt_owner-v2.pdf) warns of electrical shock, hazardous gases and immediate or delayed fire following battery damage. Contact the manufacturer or an authorized service center after significant damage or flooding. Let trained personnel arrange recovery and appropriate storage.

If there is smoke, heat, sparking, leaking fluid or an immediate danger, move away and call emergency services. Do not plug in a suspected flood-damaged EV to “see if it works.”

## A storm-damage coverage map

Check the actual event and your comprehensive coverage rather than relying on the word storm." >}}

The [California Department of Insurance](https://www.insurance.ca.gov/01-consumers/105-type/95-guides/01-auto/auto101.cfm) lists windstorm, flood, fire and falling objects among comprehensive examples. The [Texas Department of Insurance](https://www.tdi.texas.gov/tips/after-hail-or-windstorms.html) likewise identifies auto comprehensive as the coverage to check for hail damage.

| Storm event | Coverage to examine first | Question to ask |
|---|---|---|
| Hail dents panels or breaks glass | Comprehensive | Does a separate glass provision or deductible apply? |
| A tree falls on a parked EV | Comprehensive | Are recovery and any specialist inspections authorized? |
| Floodwater enters the vehicle | Comprehensive | Where should the vehicle be assessed and safely stored? |
| Wind-blown debris damages bodywork | Comprehensive | What inspection is needed beyond the visible panel damage? |
| You crash into an object during bad weather | Collision | How does the insurer classify the actual accident? |
| A home charger or building is damaged | Relevant property policy | Which policy covers the equipment and installation? |

This is a starting point, not a coverage ruling. Report what happened rather than selecting the description that sounds most likely to pay.

## Your home policy and auto policy do different jobs

Parking a car at home does not make home insurance a substitute for auto physical-damage cover. Similarly, an auto policy should not be assumed to insure the whole house, electrical installation or every charger.

Ask separately about the vehicle, wall-mounted charging equipment, portable charging cables, garage and other property. Ownership can matter: equipment installed in a rented home, shared parking garage or workplace may involve another party's policy.

Our guide to [home insurance and EV chargers](/blog/does-home-insurance-cover-ev-charger/) helps you separate those questions before two claims handlers each assume someone else is dealing with the equipment.

## What to do after the storm

### 1. Wait until the area is safe

Follow evacuation and emergency instructions. Downed power lines, unstable trees, moving water and damaged structures are hazards regardless of the car's powertrain. Do not return early for insurance photographs.

### 2. Notify the insurer promptly

The Texas regulator recommends reporting storm damage promptly and keeping records. Explain whether the car was submerged, struck, moved by water or simply exposed to hail. If you do not know how high the water reached, say so.

Ask for a claim reference, the handler's contact details, instructions for assessment and written confirmation of any immediate expenses authorized.

### 3. Document only from a safe position

Save photographs of the setting and damage when safe, the approximate time and location, and any receipts. Preserve existing warning-message screenshots without entering or reconnecting to an unsafe vehicle.

Do not open battery enclosures, remove wet trim to investigate wiring or turn an owner's claim into a DIY inspection.

### 4. Coordinate recovery before approving charges

Tell the recovery operator that the vehicle is electric and describe the damage. Ask the insurer where it should be taken and whether that facility accepts damaged EVs. Towing procedures depend on the model; follow manufacturer guidance rather than a universal rule.

### 5. Keep a running cost log

List transport, recovery, storage and other expenses, but do not mistake keeping a receipt for guaranteed reimbursement. Rental benefits and storage allowances can have limits. See our [repair-delay, rental and storage guide](/blog/ev-insurance-repair-delays-rental-storage/).

## Does a flooded EV automatically become a total loss?

{{< editorial-image name="ev-flood-assessment" alt="Illustration of an electric crossover on a dry elevated inspection area after a storm" caption="A suspected flood-damaged EV needs a safe assessment plan. Do not start or charge it simply to test whether it still works." >}}

No universal rule says that every wet EV must be written off. Nor is a vehicle safe merely because it powers on.

The assessment depends on the exposure, contamination, component condition, manufacturer procedures, repair feasibility, valuation and applicable rules. Let the qualified assessor establish the damage and the insurer explain its financial decision.

If a total loss is proposed, request the valuation and deductions. The outstanding loan does not set the vehicle's value. Our [loan balance versus vehicle value guide](/blog/ev-loan-balance-vs-value-calculator-guide/) explains why a covered claim can still leave a finance question.

## Before the next warning: five checks worth making

Check whether comprehensive cover is listed for the correct vehicle, the deductible you would pay and whether you could afford it. Confirm how to contact claims and roadside assistance if your normal phone or internet service is disrupted.

Find the manufacturer's emergency and towing guidance before you need it. Store a copy of your policy and vehicle details somewhere accessible without the car.

If a storm is approaching, follow official evacuation instructions and any manufacturer advice. Do not drive through floodwater to move the car somewhere that merely looks higher.

Finally, ask about rental coverage before a catastrophe, not after. A regional event can affect repair capacity and vehicle availability, but no one can promise your particular repair time in advance.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is hurricane damage always one deductible?

Do not assume so. Ask how the insurer defines the occurrence and which coverages and deductibles apply. A homeowner's hurricane deductible should not simply be copied across to an auto claim.

### Does charging in rain mean floodwater is safe?

No. [NHTSA](https://www.nhtsa.gov/vehicle-safety/electric-and-hybrid-vehicles) distinguishes normal weather-resistant charging from the hazards of damaged or flooded equipment. Follow manufacturer instructions and never use visibly damaged charging equipment.

### Will a battery warranty pay for storm damage?

A warranty and insurance are different contracts. Do not assume an external flood or impact is a manufacturing defect. Read [battery warranty versus insurance](/blog/ev-battery-warranty-vs-insurance/).

### What if coverage is refused?

Request the reason and exact policy clause in writing. Use the insurer's review process, then your state regulator or qualified adviser where needed.

## The most useful decision is made before the storm

You cannot control the weather. You can know which policy covers the car, where your documents are and whom to call before an unsafe vehicle starts accumulating unapproved recovery or storage charges.
