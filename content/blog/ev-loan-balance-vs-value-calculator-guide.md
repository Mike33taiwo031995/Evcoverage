You can know your monthly payment without knowing whether your EV is worth more or less than the remaining loan. Those are different questions. The first concerns your payment schedule; the second compares a debt figure with a vehicle-value estimate.

Our calculator makes the subtraction visible, but its usefulness depends on the inputs and how you interpret them. This guide explains the arithmetic, the records behind each entry and the difference between an estimated shortfall and insurance protection. Finance and insurance examples use U.S. sources unless stated otherwise.

**Short answer:** Enter the loan balance and an estimated vehicle value in the same currency. The calculator subtracts value from balance and displays the difference; it does not appraise the EV, obtain a lender payoff, predict a total-loss settlement or decide whether GAP applies. Treat the output as a question to investigate, not a recommendation to buy a product. [1][2][3]

## Understand what the calculator measures

The [homepage loan-versus-value calculator](/#eh-tools) uses two amounts you supply. It does not ask a lender or vehicle-pricing service to verify either entry, and choosing a currency does not perform an exchange.

The underlying calculation is:

**Entered loan balance − entered estimated vehicle value = difference.**

A positive result means the entered debt figure exceeds the entered value. A zero result means they match. A negative calculation means the entered value is higher; the interface displays the absolute difference with a label explaining that direction. [1]

That is a useful snapshot of your assumptions. It is not a complete affordability calculation, a depreciation forecast or a calculation of the amount payable under any insurance agreement.

## Get a current debt figure from the right document

Use a recent lender record and retain its date. Do not substitute the original amount financed, the purchase price or the monthly payment for the current amount you intend to compare.

The CFPB explains that a loan payoff amount can differ from an outstanding statement balance because of interest, fees or other reasons. If your question is what it would take to clear the debt on a specific date, ask the lender for the appropriate payoff figure. [2]

Label your record clearly: “statement balance as of this date” and “payoff quote for this date” are not interchangeable. If you run both scenarios, identify each rather than presenting a changed result as an error.

The homepage field is labelled current loan balance. Entering a separately obtained payoff is your own scenario, not evidence that the calculator requested or verified it.

## Choose a value estimate that answers your question

Ask what kind of value you are using. A trade-in estimate, an advertised selling price and an insurer's assessed actual cash value need not answer the same question.

The CFPB recommends researching vehicle values and considering information such as comparable listings and dealer estimates when evaluating a vehicle with an existing loan. California's insurance guide separately explains actual cash value in its own policy and state context. [2][4]

Keep the source, date and vehicle assumptions with the number. A value for a different variant, condition or location is not automatically a sound estimate for your EV.

Avoid changing the figure simply until the result looks comfortable. If several credible estimates differ, record that range of uncertainty and test clearly labelled scenarios instead of claiming one entry is a guaranteed sale price.

## Keep the two entries comparable

Use the same currency and a reasonably consistent date. The calculator does not translate a foreign-currency loan, fetch exchange rates or resolve differences between market definitions.

| Input or output | Useful interpretation | What it does not prove |
|---|---|---|
| Current loan balance | Debt figure entered from your record | Amount required to settle on any future day |
| Vehicle estimate | Value assumption entered for a purpose | A guaranteed sale or insurance settlement |
| Positive difference | Entered balance is higher | Eligibility for GAP or another product |
| Zero difference | The entries match | No future financial exposure |
| Value above balance | Entered value exceeds entered debt | Cash immediately available to spend |
| Currency selection | How the entries are labelled | Currency conversion or local legal coverage |

Keep the comparison's purpose beside the result. A renewal check, possible sale and already-open insurance claim may require different supporting documents.

If the correct inputs are not yet available, the useful next action is to obtain them. A precise-looking output cannot repair uncertain assumptions.

## A hypothetical example of all three results

The following amounts are invented solely to illustrate subtraction. They are not vehicle valuations, quotes, lending terms, estimates of loss or research findings.

| Hypothetical balance | Hypothetical value | Arithmetic | Meaning of the entries |
|---|---|---|---|
| 24,000 | 20,000 | 24,000 − 20,000 = 4,000 | Balance is higher |
| 20,000 | 20,000 | 20,000 − 20,000 = 0 | Entries are level |
| 18,000 | 20,000 | 18,000 − 20,000 = −2,000 | Value is higher |

Use one currency throughout each example. The tool displays the magnitude with wording about which side is higher; a displayed positive amount must be read with that label. [1]

Now change only the value assumption in your own worksheet. The result changes because the input changes—not because the site has discovered a new market price for your car.

## Separate negative equity from an insurance claim

The CFPB uses “negative equity” for owing more than a vehicle's trade-in value. It warns that rolling an existing balance into a new loan can increase the new loan's cost. That is a financing issue, not itself evidence of an insured event. [2]

Likewise, the calculator's positive difference does not prove that your EV has suffered a loss or that a claim should be filed. No accident, theft or policy interpretation is part of the subtraction.

If you are considering a sale or trade, discuss the current debt and payoff process with the lender. This article does not recommend refinancing, early repayment or carrying old debt into a new agreement.

For questions after an actual insured loss, see our [EV total-loss and GAP guide](/blog/ev-battery-total-loss-gap-coverage/) rather than treating the planning tool as a settlement statement.

## Understand where GAP may fit

The CFPB describes GAP as an optional product intended to address the difference between the loan and the insurer's payment when a car is stolen or totaled. It also warns about restrictions and the need to compare products and coverage. [3]

That definition is not “anything the calculator displays.” The agreement's eligible debt, exclusions, conditions and the primary insurer's actual payment still need checking.

Ask which written agreement you already hold before considering another product. Keep its cost separate from the auto premium so two unlike packages are not mistaken for equivalent prices.

Our [quote-comparison guide](/blog/how-to-compare-ev-insurance-quotes/) explains how to retain that distinction. The calculator cannot decide whether a particular GAP product is appropriate for you.

## Do not confuse lender requirements with optional products

The CFPB explains that lenders commonly require collision and comprehensive coverage to protect a financed vehicle. It also distinguishes other insurance and credit-protection products encountered during financing. [5]

If a product is described as required, ask where the requirement appears and confirm it with the lender. Do not infer it from a positive calculator result or from the fact that the car is electric.

The NAIC's shopping tool helps organise questions about selected coverage and costs. Use the actual documents rather than an unlabelled figure bundled into a payment. [6]

The financial comparison and the coverage decision should therefore have separate records. One shows what was entered; the other shows the terms of a contract and any professional explanation you obtained.

## Save a dated snapshot, not a permanent verdict

As an editorial recordkeeping suggestion, save the input sources, their dates and the reason you ran the comparison. Record any uncertainty about condition, outstanding debt or the meaning of the value estimate.

If a claim later occurs, keep the insurer's valuation and lender documents separately. The NAIC advises keeping claim records and obtaining written explanations of decisions; a screenshot of a planning calculation does not replace that evidence. [7]

For a systematic filing method, use the [EV claim-document checklist](/blog/ev-insurance-claim-documents-checklist/). Distinguish your calculation from a quote or assessment provided by another organisation.

Revisit the arithmetic when the inputs materially change. This guide does not invent a universal review interval or imply that last month's estimated difference remains correct today.

## Know when the tool has reached its limit

Stop treating the output as an answer when your question becomes contractual: which loss is insured, how settlement is calculated, which debt qualifies or whether a lender will accept a figure.

Take those questions to the insurer, lender or product provider, with the relevant document. For a U.S. insurance issue, the NAIC directory identifies state insurance departments; their role is different from a lender's finance enquiry. [8]

The guide to [using all the homepage tools](/blog/how-to-use-ev-insurance-tools/) shows how the loan calculation fits alongside quote comparison and regional information.

A good result from this tool is not necessarily reassurance. It can be a clear, limited question: “Which value did I use, what do I owe as of this date, and what should I verify next?”

## Frequently asked questions

**Does the calculator tell me whether to buy GAP?**

No. It subtracts user-entered amounts. GAP has separate terms and eligibility conditions, described by the CFPB; the output is not a product recommendation. [1][3]

**Should I use my monthly payment as the loan balance?**

No. The payment is not the remaining balance. Obtain the relevant current debt information from the lender and identify whether it is a statement balance or a payoff amount. [2]

**Why might my payoff differ from the statement?**

The CFPB explains that interest, charges or other factors can make payoff different. Ask the lender for a figure applicable to the date and purpose of your enquiry. [2]

**Does the tool estimate my EV's current market value?**

No. You enter that assumption. Research an appropriate value and retain its source; an insurer's settlement assessment remains a separate question. [1][2][4]

**What does value above balance mean?**

It means the entered value exceeds the entered debt figure. It does not establish a guaranteed sale price, available cash or an insurance benefit. Read the direction label with the displayed amount. [1]

**Can I compare different currencies?**

Not within this calculation. The tool provides one currency label for both entries and does not convert either amount. Mixed-currency inputs do not create a meaningful result. [1]

**Can I use the result as a total-loss settlement?**

No. Keep the insurer's actual decision and valuation, and the lender's relevant debt records. Claim decisions require the appropriate evidence and contract, not only planning arithmetic. [4][7]
