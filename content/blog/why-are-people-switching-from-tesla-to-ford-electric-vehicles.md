Tesla built the modern U.S. electric vehicle market, and for years it had a big lead in charging, software and brand recognition. Today shoppers can compare EVs from Ford, Chevrolet, Hyundai, Kia, Rivian and many others, and the question "Are people switching from Tesla to Ford?" comes up often online.

The honest answer is that the data does not show a large, documented move from Tesla to Ford. What it does show is that Tesla's customer loyalty swung sharply in 2025, that Tesla still won a major loyalty award, and that a Tesla owner who wants a different vehicle has more choices than ever. Ford is one of those choices, and some buyers prefer it for specific reasons.

This guide separates what the data says from what is speculation, then compares the Ford Mustang Mach-E with the Tesla Model Y on price, range and charging, explains where the F-150 Lightning stands, and covers the insurance, repair and warranty questions to check before switching.

**Short answer:** There is no verified data showing large numbers of Tesla owners moving to Ford. Tesla's loyalty rate fell from 73% to 49.9% between June 2024 and March 2025, then partly recovered, and the data does not say where the departing buyers went. Some shoppers do choose the Mach-E for its familiar controls, dealer network, smartphone integration and Supercharger access through an adapter, but price, range and ownership costs should drive your decision.

## Are Tesla owners really switching to Ford?

Not in any way the available data can prove. Here is what is documented.

**Tesla's loyalty dropped, then partly recovered.** Reuters reported in August 2025, using S&P Global Mobility registration data, that the share of Tesla-owning households that bought another Tesla peaked at 73% in June 2024, fell to 49.9% by March 2025 and rose to 57.4% in May 2025 ([Reuters via TradingView](https://www.tradingview.com/news/reuters.com,2025:newsml_L6N3TW0GZ:0-tesla-s-brand-loyalty-collapsed-after-musk-backed-trump-data-shows/)). The same reporting says Tesla's average inflow of new customers began falling in mid-2024.

**The data does not name Ford as the main destination.** Summaries of the S&P analysis say Rivian, Polestar and Cadillac were gaining former Tesla customers, and that Tesla had been gaining fewer than two households for every one it lost, down from a ratio of about five to one ([CBT News](https://www.cbtnews.com/teslas-u-s-loyalty-shifts-after-musks-trump-endorsement-sp-data-shows/)). Loyalty measures only the owners who stayed, so a lower rate does not show where the rest went.

**Tesla still won a loyalty award.** S&P Global Mobility named Tesla the winner of "Overall Loyalty to Make" in its 30th annual awards, announced January 14, 2026, based on 13.6 million new retail registrations from October 2024 through September 2025 ([S&P Global Mobility](https://press.spglobal.com/2026-01-14-S-P-Global-Mobility-2025-Loyalty-Awards-Reveal-Divergent-Paths-to-Customer-Retention-General-Motors-and-Tesla-Secure-Top-Honors)).

**In electric trucks, the flow runs toward Tesla.** An S&P Global Mobility analysis published May 28, 2026 found that, outside their own manufacturer, the top vehicle acquired by electric-truck households was the Tesla Model Y, and that migration between Ford and GM electric trucks was under 1% of return-to-market volume ([S&P Global Mobility](https://www.spglobal.com/automotive-insights/en/blogs/2026/05/six-in-ten-electric-truck-households-defect-to-another-brand)).

The takeaway: loyalty is fragmented. A Tesla owner who buys a Ford is responding to a better match for their needs, not following a proven trend.

## Why some shoppers still consider Ford

These are common reasons buyers give for comparing the two, not measured trends.

- **Changing needs.** A Model 3 owner may now want a larger family vehicle or a pickup.
- **Familiar controls.** The Mach-E pairs a 15.5-inch touchscreen with a separate 10.2-inch digital instrument display, and wireless Apple CarPlay and Android Auto come standard ([Edmunds](https://www.edmunds.com/ford/mustang-mach-e/), [U.S. News](https://cars.usnews.com/cars-trucks/ford/mustang-mach-e)).
- **Dealer network.** Some buyers like being able to visit a local dealership. Service quality still varies by dealer, so a traditional network is not automatically better.
- **Charging access.** Ford owners can now use Tesla Superchargers, which narrows what used to be Tesla's biggest advantage (details below).

## Mustang Mach-E vs. Tesla Model Y

Both are five-seat electric crossovers. This table uses 2026 model-year figures from Edmunds and U.S. News. Specs vary by trim, and prices change often.

| | Ford Mustang Mach-E | Tesla Model Y |

| Starting MSRP (before destination) | $37,795 (Select) | $39,990 (Standard) |

### Which is better?

Neither wins outright. Range comparisons only make sense trim for trim, because both brands list several configurations with different battery and drivetrain combinations. A buyer who values Tesla's integrated software and charging experience may prefer the Model Y. A buyer who wants physical controls, smartphone mirroring and a local dealership may prefer the Mach-E. Test-drive both.

## The F-150 Lightning: check availability first

Ford ended F-150 Lightning production in late 2025. Reporting based on Ford's statements says existing owners keep warranty coverage, service, parts and software support ([autoevolution](https://www.autoevolution.com/news/ford-pulled-the-plug-on-the-f-150-lightning-but-tells-owners-not-to-worry-we-ve-got-your-back-275481.html)). That makes the Lightning mainly a used-market or remaining-inventory option. If you are weighing a used one, our guide to [Ford's EV battery warranty](/blog/ford-electric-vehicle-battery-warranty/) explains the 8-year/100,000-mile coverage and what a second owner receives.

For any electric pickup, compare towing capacity, payload, real range while towing, charging along your route, insurance and battery warranty. Range can drop significantly under heavy towing.

## Charging: Ford owners can use Tesla Superchargers

Tesla opened its Supercharger network to Ford EV owners in the U.S. and Canada, and Ford says F-150 Lightning and Mustang Mach-E customers were the first non-Tesla owners to gain access across both countries ([Electrek](https://electrek.co/?p=351841)). Ford offered a free CCS-to-NACS adapter, and Electrek later reported that Ford had shipped all 140,000 of the complimentary adapters, with Superchargers accounting for almost 20% of public charging stops by Ford EV owners in one February ([Electrek](https://electrek.co/?p=405184)).

Caveats to check before you rely on it:

- At launch, Ford said the adapter worked only at select V3 and V4 Superchargers, and owners needed the latest software update ([Drive Tesla Canada](https://driveteslacanada.ca/news/tesla-officially-opens-supercharger-network-to-ford-ev-owners-in-canada-and-us)). By March 2025, electrive reported that Ford owners with the adapter had access to more than 20,000 Superchargers in the U.S. ([electrive](https://www.electrive.com/2025/03/13/ford-evs-gain-access-to-thousands-of-tesla-superchargers-in-the-us)). Still, use Tesla's map or the Ford charging app to confirm each location.
- Charging speed depends on the vehicle, battery temperature, state of charge and the station's output.
- Ford owners may juggle different apps and payment systems from Tesla owners. If you drive long distances, plan a real route before you switch.

## Pricing, incentives and total cost

Starting prices are close, and actual transaction prices depend on trim, inventory and dealer arrangements.

The federal clean vehicle tax credits for new and used EVs ended September 30, 2025, according to the IRS ([IRS bulletin](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/USIRS/bulletins/3f4df63)). Check for state and utility incentives, which change over time.

A fair comparison looks past the sticker price:

| Cost | What to compare |
|---|---|
| Purchase price | Negotiated price, destination and fees |
| Financing | Interest rate and loan term |
| Insurance | Quotes for the exact vehicle and trim |
| Electricity | Home and public charging costs |
| Maintenance | Tires, brakes, fluids, inspections |
| Repairs | Parts, labor and diagnostic time |
| Depreciation | Expected resale value |
| Warranty | Remaining manufacturer coverage |

## Interior, technology and driver assistance

Tesla's approach is minimalist, with most functions on the central screen. Ford combines a large screen with a separate instrument display and, on the Mach-E, wireless smartphone mirroring. Some drivers like Tesla's clean cabin; others prefer more conventional controls.

Both brands sell driver-assistance features, such as Ford's BlueCruise and Tesla's Full Self-Driving (Supervised). These are not self-driving systems. The driver stays responsible, and feature availability, conditions and subscription terms vary, so test them yourself instead of relying on marketing.

## Brand perception

Reuters reported that some analysts linked the loyalty drop to Elon Musk's growing political involvement, alongside an aging product lineup and stiffer competition. That is analyst interpretation, not proof of why any individual buyer switched. Some people want a different vehicle, better pricing or a different service experience, and Ford's long history in the U.S. market makes an electric Ford feel familiar to some. Neither brand's reputation guarantees a better ownership experience, because the specific vehicle and the dealership matter.

## Is switching from Tesla to Ford financially sensible?

Only if the numbers work for your situation. Before you decide:

1. Get written price quotes and insurance quotes for the exact trims.
2. Value your current Tesla and compare the trade-in or resale offers.
3. Check whether Supercharger access, with an adapter where needed, covers your routes.
4. Compare charging at home and on the road.
5. Check warranty coverage and remaining time on both vehicles.
6. Test-drive both, including the driver-assistance and touchscreen features you will actually use.

## Frequently asked questions

**Are Tesla owners really switching to Ford?**
There is no verified data showing a large move from Tesla to Ford. Tesla's loyalty rate fell sharply in 2025 and partly recovered, but the data does not identify Ford as the main destination.

**Did Tesla's loyalty rate really fall?**
According to S&P Global Mobility data reported by Reuters, it fell from 73% in June 2024 to 49.9% in March 2025 and recovered to 57.4% in May 2025. S&P also named Tesla its Overall Loyalty to Make winner in January 2026.

**Can a Ford Mustang Mach-E use Tesla Superchargers?**
Yes, with an approved adapter, at compatible locations. Ford launched access in the U.S. and Canada and offered a free adapter to owners at the time. Check compatibility at each station.

**Which is cheaper, the Mach-E or the Model Y?**
The 2026 Mach-E Select starts at $37,795 and the Model Y Standard at $39,990, both before destination and other charges. Your real cost depends on trim, incentives, insurance and resale value.

**Is the F-150 Lightning still being built?**
No. Ford ended production in late 2025, and existing owners keep warranty and service support according to reporting on Ford's statements.

**Is there still a federal EV tax credit?**
No. The IRS says the federal credits for new and used EVs ended on September 30, 2025. State and utility incentives may still apply.

**Will insurance cost more on one than the other?**
It depends on the vehicle, your driving record, location and insurer, so get quotes for both before deciding.
