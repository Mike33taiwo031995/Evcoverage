Two EV owners install the exact same Level 2 home charger. One files a claim after a lightning strike fries the unit and gets paid out within two weeks. The other files an almost identical claim and gets denied. The difference usually isn't luck — it's whether the charger was properly declared, permanently installed, and covered under the right part of the policy.

According to industry guidance from insurers including Liberty Mutual and Allstate, a permanently installed home charger is typically treated as part of your dwelling or personal property, and is covered under homeowners insurance when damaged by a covered peril like fire, lightning, or vandalism — subject to your deductible and policy limits. That's the good news. But "typically" and "permanently installed" are both doing a lot of work in that sentence, and that's where most owners get caught out.

### The two conditions that actually decide it

Coverage generally comes down to two things: **installation type**, and **whether the damage came from the home side or the vehicle side**.

- **Permanently installed (hardwired Level 2 charger):** Usually covered under homeowners insurance as a fixture, similar to a water heater or HVAC unit.
- **Portable Level 1 charger you plug in and unplug:** More likely to be treated as personal property or, in some cases, as vehicle equipment — which can fall into a gap between home and auto policy.
- **Damage caused by your home's electrical system** (a wiring fault, a power surge, a storm): Homeowners insurance territory.
- **Damage caused by your vehicle** (backing into the charger, a collision with the unit): Your auto policy's collision coverage is more likely to apply.

### Why some claims still get denied

Even a permanently installed charger can run into trouble if:

1. **It was never declared to the insurer.** Adding a charger is an electrical modification to your home — insurers generally expect you to report it, and an undeclared charger can complicate a claim.
2. **It gets misclassified as "automotive equipment."** Some adjusters initially treat charging equipment as an extension of the vehicle rather than the home, especially if the paperwork doesn't clearly document it as a fixed installation.
3. **State rules require specific coverage you don't have.** California and Oregon, for example, require homeowners and condo associations to carry liability coverage for EV charging stations — meaning "having home insurance" isn't automatically the same as "having the right home insurance" everywhere else.

<li>Is your charger permanently hardwired (not just plugged into an outlet)?</li>
<li>Did you tell your insurer about it when it was installed?</li>

</ol>
If you answered "no" or "not sure" to #1 or #2, that's the gap worth closing before you ever need to file a claim.

### Frequently asked questions

**Does auto insurance ever cover a home charger?**
Generally no — if your home charging station is damaged by fire, weather, or a similar event, that's a homeowners/renters insurance claim, not an auto insurance claim. Auto insurance becomes relevant mainly when your vehicle itself causes the damage.

**Do I need to tell my insurer I installed a charger?**
Yes. Since it's an electrical modification to your home, most insurers expect it to be disclosed, and it may affect your dwelling coverage limits.

**Is a portable Level 1 charger treated differently?**
Often, yes — since it isn't a fixed installation, it's more likely to be treated as personal property, and coverage can be murkier than for a hardwired Level 2 unit.
