One of the biggest everyday differences between an electric car and a gasoline car is routine maintenance. If you have spent years paying for oil changes, oil filters and spark plugs, it is fair to ask whether an EV needs the same services.

The short answer is no. A fully electric vehicle has no combustion engine, so it has no engine oil to change. But "no oil changes" does not mean "no maintenance." EVs still have brakes, tires, suspension, coolant systems, cabin air filters and, in many designs, specialized drivetrain lubricant. Intervals also differ by manufacturer, so one brand's schedule should never be applied to another.

This guide explains why EVs skip oil changes, what they need instead, how Tesla, Ford and Chevrolet describe their schedules, how hybrids differ, and what maintenance costs look like.

**Short answer:** No. A battery electric vehicle does not need engine oil changes, oil filters or spark plugs. It still needs tire rotations, brake inspections and fluid checks, cabin air filters and, on a manufacturer-specific schedule, coolant service. Hybrids and plug-in hybrids do still need oil changes, because they have gasoline engines.

## Why electric cars don't need engine oil

A gasoline engine has pistons, a crankshaft, valves and bearings that move under heat and friction. Engine oil lubricates these parts, helps manage heat and carries contaminants to the filter. Over time the oil breaks down and has to be replaced.

An electric car works differently. It stores electricity in a battery and uses an electric motor to turn the wheels. With no combustion engine, there is no engine oil to drain, so a fully electric vehicle does not need:

- Engine oil changes and oil filters
- Spark plugs
- Engine air filters
- Fuel-system servicing
- Exhaust-system maintenance
- Engine timing belt or chain service

Chevrolet says its EVs do not require oil changes, transmission flushes, engine belt and air filter replacement or spark plugs ([Chevrolet](https://www.chevrolet.com/electric/living-electric/complete-care)). The U.S. Department of Energy says all-electric vehicles typically need less maintenance than conventional vehicles because the battery, motor and electronics need little to no regular maintenance, there are fewer fluids such as engine oil to maintain, and there are far fewer moving parts ([DOE AFDC](https://afdc.energy.gov/files/u/publication/electric_vehicles.pdf)).

## Do EVs use any oil or lubricant?

Some do. An EV's motor does not need engine oil, but the drivetrain can have gears and bearings. Many EVs use a reduction gear or drive-unit assembly between the motor and the wheels, and some of these use specialized gear oil or drivetrain fluid. Whether yours does, and how often it needs service, depends on the design. For example, Ford dealer schedules for the 2025 Mustang Mach-E list electric-drive-assembly fluid at 10 years or 150,000 miles ([Mahon Ford](https://www.mahonford.com/2025-ford-mustang-mach-e-maintenance-schedule.htm)), but other brands differ. Check your own manual.

Do not assume ordinary automatic transmission fluid is suitable for an electric drive unit. Fluids for EV drivetrains can have different specifications, and using the wrong one can damage components. Use only what the manufacturer approves.

## What fluids do electric cars still need?

| Fluid | Purpose | Needed in a fully electric car? |
|---|---|---|
| Engine oil | Lubricates a combustion engine | No |
| Battery and motor coolant | Helps regulate battery and component temperature | Many EVs use it |
| Brake fluid | Transmits hydraulic braking pressure | Yes |
| Windshield washer fluid | Cleans the windscreen | Yes |
| Drive-unit gear oil or fluid | Lubricates gears and bearings | Some designs |

### Battery and thermal-management coolant

Many EVs use liquid cooling to keep the battery, motor and power electronics at suitable temperatures, which matters for performance, charging and reliability. Coolant intervals differ a lot. Ford's EV guide recommends changing the coolant at 200,000 miles ([Ford](https://www.ford.com/electric/?gnav=header-electrified-bev)). Other brands specify other intervals, and some dealer-published schedules for the same Ford model list different mileages, which is a good reason to confirm the schedule for your exact model year in the owner's manual.

### Brake fluid

EVs use hydraulic friction brakes, and regenerative braking supplements them but does not replace them. Brake fluid absorbs moisture over time, which can reduce braking performance and encourage corrosion. Tesla recommends testing brake fluid for contamination every 4 years and replacing it as needed, and says heavy brake use from towing, mountain descents or performance driving, especially in hot and humid climates, may call for more frequent checks ([Tesla](https://www.tesla.com/support/vehicle-maintenance)).

### Washer fluid

Check the reservoir and refill it as needed.

## What maintenance does an EV need instead of oil changes?

### Tires

Tires are the most important recurring item. EVs are often heavier than comparable gasoline cars and deliver torque instantly, which can increase tire wear, though driving style, pressure and alignment also matter. Ford's EV guide says EV tires typically last around 30,000 to 40,000 miles, varying with the vehicle and how it is driven and maintained ([Ford](https://www.ford.com/electric/?gnav=header-electrified-bev)).

Rotation intervals vary:

- **Tesla:** every 6,250 miles, or sooner if tread depth differs by 2/32 inch or more between tires ([Tesla](https://www.tesla.com/support/vehicle-maintenance)).
- **Ford:** every 10,000 miles ([Ford](https://www.ford.com/electric/?gnav=header-electrified-bev)).
- **Chevrolet:** every 7,500 miles ([Chevrolet](https://www.chevrolet.com/certified-service/ev-maintenance)).

### Brakes

Because regenerative braking does some of the slowing, brake pads often last longer than on a comparable gasoline car, and the DOE says EV brake systems generally last longer than conventional ones. That does not mean brakes can be ignored. Pads, rotors, calipers, hoses, the parking brake and fluid still need inspection. In regions where roads are salted in winter, guides summarizing Tesla's recommendations suggest cleaning and lubricating the calipers yearly or every 12,500 miles ([The Motor Guy](https://themotorguy.com/tesla-maintenance-schedule-service-costs/)).

### Cabin air filter

An EV has no engine air filter, but it still has a cabin air filter that cleans the air entering the passenger compartment. Tesla recommends replacing it every 2 years on the Model 3 and Model Y and every 3 years on the Model S and Model X ([Tesla](https://www.tesla.com/support/vehicle-maintenance)). Ford says to add a cabin filter change at 40,000 miles and every 20,000 miles afterward ([Ford](https://www.ford.com/electric/?gnav=header-electrified-bev)). Chevrolet's EV schedule page lists replacing it annually or every 12,000 miles ([Chevrolet](https://www.chevrolet.com/support/vehicle/maintenance/schedule/about-ev)).

### Suspension and steering

Ball joints, tie rods, bushings, control arms, wheel bearings and shocks wear over time. Have them inspected on schedule and whenever you notice noise, vibration or handling changes.

### Software updates

Many EV systems depend on software. Install manufacturer updates when prompted. They do not replace mechanical maintenance, but they are part of keeping a modern EV in good order.

## How often should an EV be serviced?

There is no universal schedule. It depends on the manufacturer, model, year, cooling design and how you drive. Here is how three brands describe theirs, based on their own published pages. Always confirm the schedule for your exact vehicle.

| Item | Tesla (Model 3/Y) | Ford (EV guide) | Chevrolet |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tire rotation | Every 6,250 miles or at 2/32 in tread difference | Every 10,000 miles | Every 7,500 miles |
| Cabin air filter | Every 2 years | At 40,000 miles, then every 20,000 | Annually or every 12,000 miles (EV schedule page) |
| Brake fluid | Test every 4 years, replace as needed | Not listed in Ford's guide | Varies by model |
| Coolant | Check your manual | Replace at 200,000 miles | Varies by model |
| General service | Item-based intervals | Basic maintenance every 10,000 miles at an EV-certified dealer | Varies by model |

Ford's guide recommends bringing the vehicle to a Ford EV-certified dealership for basic maintenance every 10,000 miles. If you are weighing Ford against another brand, see [how Ford handles EV battery warranty](/blog/ford-electric-vehicle-battery-warranty/), because warranty terms also tie to approved service.

Tesla's Model S and X use a 3-year cabin filter interval, so do not apply the Model 3 and Y figure to them. Likewise, a coolant interval published for one Ford should not be assumed for a Tesla or a Chevrolet. The owner's manual and the manufacturer's service information are the references that matter.

## Do hybrids and plug-in hybrids need oil changes?

Yes. This trips up many shoppers, because not every "electrified" car is fully electric.

| Vehicle type | Has a gasoline engine? | Needs engine oil changes? |
|---|---|---|
| Battery electric (BEV) | No | No |
| Hybrid (HEV) | Yes | Yes |
| Plug-in hybrid (PHEV) | Yes | Yes |
| Extended-range EV (EREV) | Yes, as a generator | Check the manual |
| Gasoline or diesel | Yes | Yes |

The DOE says maintenance needs for plug-in hybrids and hybrids are similar to those of conventional vehicles ([DOE AFDC](https://afdc.energy.gov/files/u/publication/electric_vehicles.pdf)). If a car has a combustion engine, its oil maintenance does not disappear because it also has an electric motor. To understand which Fords are fully electric and which are hybrids, read [what electric vehicles Ford makes](/blog/what-electric-vehicles-does-ford-make/).

## Are electric cars cheaper to maintain?

Generally, yes for scheduled maintenance. A 2021 Argonne National Laboratory study for the Department of Energy estimated scheduled maintenance at 6.1 cents per mile for a battery electric vehicle versus 10.1 cents per mile for a gasoline vehicle ([Government Fleet](https://www.government-fleet.com/news/report-evs-cost-less-to-maintain)). The saving comes from skipping oil, timing belts, oxygen sensors and spark plugs. This is a nationwide average from one study, not a guarantee for your car.

### What you still pay for

- Tires, rotation and alignment
- Brake inspections and brake fluid
- Cabin air filters
- Coolant on its schedule
- Wiper blades and washer fluid
- Suspension and steering repairs
- Diagnostics and electrical repair when needed

### Why EV repairs can still be expensive

Lower routine maintenance does not mean every repair is cheap. High-voltage and electronic problems usually need trained technicians, and accident repairs can cost more. In Mitchell's Q2 2026 U.S. data, the average repairable collision claim was $5,684 for a battery electric vehicle versus $4,955 for a gasoline vehicle ([Mitchell](https://www.mitchell.com/insights/article/auto-physical-damage/plugged-in-ev-collision-insights-q2-2026)); see [EV collision repair costs and claims](/blog/electric-vehicle-collision-repair/). For the electrical side, our guide to [vehicle electrical repair costs](/blog/vehicle-electrical-repair-cost/) shows published U.S. estimates, and [how to find a qualified EV repair shop](/blog/electric-vehicle-repair-near-me/) explains who should work on high-voltage systems. If a battery ever needs replacing, see [EV battery replacement cost in 2026](/blog/ev-battery-replacement-cost-2026/).

Remember the full ownership picture too: insurance, charging, financing, registration and depreciation all matter, not only routine maintenance.

## Keeping your EV in good shape

1. **Follow your owner's manual,** not a generic list.
2. **Rotate tires on time** and check pressure regularly.
3. **Test or replace brake fluid** on your manufacturer's schedule.
4. **Don't ignore warning messages.** Have unusual noises, vibrations or warnings checked promptly.
5. **Use approved fluids only,** and never substitute conventional transmission fluid in an EV drive unit.
6. **Leave high-voltage work to trained technicians.**
7. **Keep service records,** which help with warranty and resale.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do electric cars need oil changes?**
No. A fully electric car has no combustion engine and no engine oil. It still needs tires, brakes, filters and fluids serviced on the manufacturer's schedule.

**Do electric cars need any oil at all?**
Some use gear oil or drivetrain fluid in the drive unit, but this is not engine oil, and intervals vary by design. Check your manual.

**Do electric cars need coolant?**
Many do, to regulate battery and motor temperature. Intervals vary widely, for example Ford's EV guide lists 200,000 miles.

**Do EVs need brake fluid?**
Yes. They use hydraulic friction brakes, and brake fluid absorbs moisture over time. Tesla recommends testing it every 4 years.

**Do hybrids need oil changes?**
Yes. Hybrids and plug-in hybrids have gasoline engines that need oil changes.

**How often should I rotate EV tires?**
It depends on the brand: Tesla says every 6,250 miles, Chevrolet 7,500 and Ford 10,000.

**Is EV maintenance really cheaper?**
Scheduled maintenance generally costs less, with a 2021 DOE-supported study estimating 6.1 cents per mile for a BEV versus 10.1 for a gasoline car. Repair and tire costs can narrow the gap.
