The floodwater has gone, but two questions remain: is your electric car safe to approach, and which insurance policy should you call? Those questions need separate answers. An insurer's willingness to consider a claim is not permission to start, charge or move the vehicle yourself.

This guide explains the U.S. insurance position, the difference between saltwater and freshwater exposure, and the steps from reporting damage to a repair or total-loss decision. State examples and manufacturer instructions are identified where used; sources were reviewed in November 2026.

**Short answer:** In the U.S., comprehensive auto coverage generally covers flood damage to an insured electric car, subject to the policy's terms and deductible; liability-only coverage is not protection for your own flooded vehicle. If flooding or submersion may have affected the vehicle, do not start or charge it to test whether it works: contact the manufacturer or an appropriately qualified service provider, and emergency services if there are warning signs. [1][2][6]

## What insurance covers a flood-damaged electric car?

Start with your auto policy's declarations page: the summary identifying your vehicle, coverages and deductibles. The Texas Department of Insurance and Maryland Insurance Administration both direct flooded-car owners to comprehensive coverage, sometimes called “other than collision.” [1][3]

California's insurance guide makes the distinction clear: comprehensive can cover flood and other non-collision events, while collision concerns physical contact with a vehicle or object. Liability coverage addresses injury or property damage you cause to others, not this kind of damage to your own car. These are state consumer explanations; your contract still matters. [2]

| Coverage or protection | What to check after a flood | What not to assume |
|---|---|---|
| Comprehensive auto coverage | Whether it covered this vehicle when the loss happened, plus the deductible and exclusions | That every expense or requested repair is approved |
| Collision coverage | Whether a separate impact also occurred and how the insurer classifies it | That collision replaces comprehensive for flood damage |
| Liability-only auto coverage | Confirm exactly which coverages you bought | That it pays for your own flooded EV |
| NFIP building or contents coverage | FEMA's vehicle exclusions | That a car parked at home becomes covered contents |
| Manufacturer's battery warranty | The applicable damage exclusions | That a long battery warranty includes flood damage |

These are coverage checks, not individual claim decisions. [1][2][4][10]

## Does home insurance or NFIP flood insurance cover the car?

Do not confuse “insurance against a flood” with the specific policy that insures a vehicle. FEMA's National Flood Insurance Program, or NFIP, summary lists most self-propelled vehicles, including cars and their parts, among excluded property. Parking the EV in an insured garage does not remove that exclusion. [4]

For the flooded vehicle itself, check the auto policy first. A damaged home charger is a separate item: describe its installation and damage to the relevant insurer rather than adding it to the car claim without asking.

Our guide to [home insurance and EV chargers](/blog/does-home-insurance-cover-ev-charger/) discusses the equipment question. It should not be read as a promise that a home policy covers flood damage to either the charger or the car.

## Saltwater versus freshwater: what changes?

Saltwater deserves particular attention. The U.S. Pipeline and Hazardous Materials Safety Administration, or PHMSA, explains that residual salt inside a battery or its components can create conductive paths, leading to short circuits, self-heating and fire. Its advisory concerns transporting EVs with potentially damaged lithium batteries. [5]

Freshwater is not an all-clear. NHTSA's flood guidance warns that flooded EVs may have high voltage and short circuits that can cause shock and fire; it does not offer a freshwater exception. Tell the service provider what type of water was involved, if known, without approaching the vehicle to investigate. [7]

PHMSA also warns that ignition of a damaged battery can occur days or weeks later. That describes a possible hazard, not the likelihood for every flooded EV, and it does not establish a waiting period after which a vehicle is safe. [5]

Report the actual exposure; the water's type alone does not decide insurance coverage.

## What should you do immediately after flooding?

Do not start or charge a suspected flooded EV as a test. NHTSA's emergency-services guidance expressly warns against starting or charging suspected flooded or submerged electric and hybrid-electric vehicles because of shock and fire hazards. [6]

Keep away from exposed electrical parts and do not attempt battery repairs. NHTSA advises treating the high-voltage battery and associated components as energised after damage; switching the vehicle off is not proof that those components are safe to touch. [8]

If you see smoke or flames, notice unusual heating, or hear popping or hissing, move away and call emergency services. Tell responders that the vehicle is electric and has been exposed to flooding; Tesla's submersion guidance identifies these warning signs and directs people to local first responders. [9]

If there are no obvious warning signs, still arrange professional assessment before operation. “It looks dry” and “the screen turns on” are not the inspection clearance Tesla's guidance calls for after prolonged submersion. [9]

## How should towing and storage be arranged?

Tell the insurer and recovery operator that this is a flood-exposed EV, not simply a car that will not start. Provide the make, model, location and known exposure so they can plan recovery using the manufacturer's instructions.

NHTSA says not to park a damaged vehicle with a lithium-ion battery in a garage or within 50 feet of a structure, another vehicle or combustible material. Tesla similarly calls for safe towing away from structures and combustibles after submersion. **Do not drive or push the car yourself merely to create that separation; ask responders or a qualified recovery provider how to handle the location safely.** [7][9]

PHMSA's advisory highlights the need to assess potentially damaged batteries before commercial transportation. Owners should disclose the flood exposure rather than arrange transport on the assumption that a dried-out vehicle is ordinary cargo. [5]

Ask the insurer which recovery and storage arrangements it authorises, what charges may be reimbursed, and who will arrange inspection. Our guide to [finding an EV-qualified repair shop](/blog/electric-vehicle-repair-near-me/) can help you prepare questions about training and manufacturer information, but confirm that the facility can handle a flood-exposed vehicle before recovery. There is no single towing method this article can prescribe for every model and condition.

## How do you document and file the claim?

Report the loss promptly. Texas and Maryland regulators advise contacting the insurer and taking photographs or video of flood damage. Only document what you can from a safe location; do not enter floodwater or approach a hazardous vehicle for better photographs. [1][3]

Useful information to organise includes:

- The vehicle's location and when you first discovered the flooding.
- Photographs already available, including any visible waterline.
- Whether the water was fresh, salt or unknown, if you know.
- Any attempts already made to start, charge or move the vehicle.
- Recovery details, receipts and service-provider reports.

Separate observations from assumptions. “Water reached the lower door” is an observation; “the battery is ruined” is a diagnosis you may not be qualified to make.

Request a claim number and written instructions on inspection and repair authorisation. If the insurer asks for cleaning or measures to limit further damage, explain that it is an EV and arrange a safe professional plan; do not treat general flooded-car drying advice as permission to work around high-voltage components yourself. [1][8]

## Will a flooded EV be repaired or declared a total loss?

Flood exposure does not let this article predict the outcome. Texas's regulator explains that insurers compare a vehicle's actual cash value with repair costs, and that modern electronics can make flood repairs expensive even when damage is not obvious. [1]

Actual cash value, or ACV, is the valuation used under the applicable policy and rules. California's guide describes ACV as fair market value unless the policy defines it otherwise. It is not automatically the original purchase price or the amount owed on finance. [2]

There is no nationwide percentage to apply to every claim. As reviewed in November 2026, Maryland's regulator says an insurer must total a vehicle when repairs exceed 75% of its value, but may also total it below that level for safety or financial reasons. That is a Maryland example, not a U.S.-wide threshold. [3]

Ask for the inspection findings, repair estimate and valuation supporting the decision. For the wider finance implications, see our guide to [EV battery damage, total loss and GAP coverage](/blog/ev-battery-total-loss-gap-coverage/).

## What might you still have to pay?

A covered loss is not necessarily a zero-cost loss. Your comprehensive deductible is the portion of the covered loss you are responsible for under the policy; California's consumer guide explains how deductibles affect physical-damage claims. Confirm the comprehensive deductible rather than assuming it matches your collision deductible. [2]

If the car is a total loss, compare the settlement explanation with the loan balance separately. Texas's flood guidance identifies GAP coverage as a possible route for the difference, but your particular policy or agreement must be checked before assuming a shortfall qualifies. [1]

For towing, storage, alternative transport and diagnostic work, ask what is authorised and what limits apply before incurring avoidable charges. This article does not establish that those expenses are included in your policy.

If a repair estimate proposes battery replacement, ask what findings support it and what work the insurer has approved. Our guide to [insurance coverage for EV battery replacement](/blog/is-ev-battery-replacement-covered-by-insurance/) explains why the cause of damage matters more than the size of the bill.

## Can the battery warranty pay instead?

A battery warranty is not automatically flood protection. Ford's U.S. 2026 battery-electric vehicle warranty guide, for example, lists immersion or flooding of the high-voltage battery assembly among excluded damage causes. That is an example from Ford's written terms, not a statement about every manufacturer's warranty. [10]

If a service provider identifies a possible defect alongside flood exposure, request the diagnosis and warranty decision in writing. An insurance refusal does not prove a defect.

The distinction is explained in our [EV battery warranty versus insurance guide](/blog/ev-battery-warranty-vs-insurance/). Keep the warranty enquiry separate from the insurance claim so each decision can be assessed against the correct document.

## What can you do before a flood warning becomes an emergency?

If it is still safe, follow local evacuation instructions and move the vehicle away from flood-prone parking. NHTSA's preparedness guidance recommends considering early evacuation and safer or higher-ground vehicle storage, while accounting for charging availability along evacuation routes. [6]

Before flooding reaches the area, follow the manufacturer's instructions for disconnecting the vehicle from charging equipment. Do not approach or handle flooded charging equipment to carry out a preparation step after the hazard has arrived. NHTSA's advice to disconnect applies ahead of storm surge or flooding. [6]

Keep policy documents and insurer contact details accessible away from the car. Confirm your existing comprehensive coverage and deductible before an incident rather than discovering their absence while reporting a loss.

Do not drive into a flooded road to protect the vehicle from damage elsewhere. NHTSA's flood-response guidance tells drivers approaching flooded roads to turn around. Personal safety takes priority over retrieving or relocating the car. [6]

## What should you ask the adjuster before agreeing to a decision?

The most useful questions connect the damage findings to the proposed payment. Ask:

- Which coverage and deductible are being applied to this event?
- Who will assess the flood exposure and high-voltage system?
- What written findings support repair or total loss?
- Which towing, inspection and storage charges are authorised?
- If repairs are approved, who will confirm the vehicle is safe to operate?
- If it is a total loss, how was the vehicle valued?

Keep the answers with the reports and estimates. If you disagree with the valuation, Texas and Maryland regulators suggest supporting your position with evidence such as comparable vehicles and dealer information rather than simply stating a higher figure. [1][3]

## Frequently asked questions

**Does liability-only insurance cover my flooded electric car?**

Not as protection for your own vehicle. California's guide distinguishes liability for damage to others from comprehensive protection for events such as flooding. Check your declarations page to establish whether comprehensive covered the EV when the loss happened. [1][2]

**Is saltwater damage automatically excluded from car insurance?**

The state guidance reviewed here identifies comprehensive as the coverage for flood damage; it does not establish a universal saltwater exclusion. Your insurer must assess the actual policy. Saltwater's particular battery hazard is a separate safety issue described by PHMSA. [1][3][5]

**Can I charge the car once the floodwater has receded?**

Do not charge a suspected flooded or submerged EV to see whether it still works. NHTSA warns against doing so, and Tesla advises inspection before operating a vehicle after prolonged submersion. Follow the relevant manufacturer's instructions and professional assessment. [6][9]

**Does freshwater flooding mean the battery is safe?**

No such assurance follows from the guidance. NHTSA warns about high-voltage and short-circuit hazards in flooded EVs without giving freshwater exposure a safe exception. Report the exposure and arrange assessment rather than testing the vehicle yourself. [7]

**Is every flood-damaged EV a total loss?**

This guide cannot make that determination. Repair costs, vehicle value, safety findings and state rules matter. Maryland's published guidance expressly allows safety or financial total-loss decisions below its stated mandatory threshold, illustrating why a single percentage is not the whole decision. [1][3]

**Does FEMA flood insurance cover a car inside my garage?**

FEMA's NFIP coverage summary excludes most self-propelled vehicles, including cars and their parts. Do not assume the building or contents policy covers the EV simply because it was parked inside. Check the vehicle's comprehensive auto coverage. [1][4]

**Can a flood-damaged battery become hazardous later?**

Yes. PHMSA warns that ignition can occur days or weeks after battery damage. That is not a prediction for every flooded vehicle, but it is a reason to follow professional recovery and storage instructions rather than treating a period without smoke as proof of safety. [5]
